# Market check: Vouched

Checked 2026-10-15.

**Claim:** a student-controlled record where each extracurricular claim is confirmed by a named person at the
source, paid for by colleges and programs reaching opted-in students.

## What exists

| Who | What | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| [Parchment](https://trainingindustry.com/press-release/learning-technologies/instructure-acquires-parchment-for-795-million/) | Verified transcripts, diplomas, certificates. Bought by Instructure in 2023 for about $795M net (~$835M with tax benefit); ~$115M expected 2024 revenue. | Precedent: verification of the academic record is a large business. Does not cover activities. |
| [College Board Student Search](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/east/2024/02/14/760729.htm) | Sold student names to colleges at roughly $0.40–$0.50 each; about $75M revenue from Search in 2021. NY settlement Feb 2024 ($750K, stop monetizing NY students' test-day data); a [class action](https://news.wttw.com/node/29568) alleges sales without consent. | Proves colleges pay to reach students, and that doing it without consent is now a legal liability. Vouched's model is opt-in messaging, never selling data. |
| How colleges verify today | No national database; follow-up checks are rare and reserved for unusual claims ([Ivy Institute](https://theivyinst.org/blog/how-do-colleges-verify-extracurricular-activities-outside-your-high-school), [Spark Admissions](https://www.sparkadmissions.com/blog/do-colleges-verify-your-extracurriculars/)). | The gap. |
| [RaiseMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RaiseMe) | Students log achievements for micro-scholarships from partner colleges. | Adjacent: colleges paying around student achievements, self-reported. |

## What's open

We found no product that verifies extracurricular claims at the source and lets students share the verified record.

## Risks to check next

- Student privacy law (FERPA generally does not cover a service students sign up for themselves, but COPPA for under-13s, state laws like
  California's SOPIPA for anything sold to schools, and the College Board precedent all shape the paid side).
- Will admissions offices read a third-party link? Talk to 5 admissions readers before building the paid side.
