# Market check

The user wants ideas that are genuinely unique and can make real money. Checking is cheap; building something that
already exists, or claiming a market fact that is wrong, is expensive.

## Procedure

1. **Name the claim precisely.** "AI booking for local businesses" is too broad to check. "A neutral booking layer
   that AI assistants call for shops with no booking software, confirmed by SMS" is checkable.
2. **Search three angles in one turn** (WebSearch, standard mode first):
   - the product as a buyer would describe it ("app that verifies extracurricular activities for college")
   - the closest known category plus the twist ("Parchment for extracurriculars", "Kilimo for cities")
   - the money: who already pays for this today, and how much (acquisitions, revenue, pricing pages, settlements)
   Use extended mode only when standard results are thin or stale.
3. **Classify each hit:** direct competitor / adjacent (same buyer, different product) / precedent (same model,
   different market) / irrelevant. Note the date: a 2009 product is precedent, not competition.
4. **Write it down** in the app's `docs/market.md`: the claim, each relevant hit with a link and one line, what is
   still open, and the date checked. Update it rather than starting over.
5. **Phrase results honestly** everywhere they are reused: "we found no one doing X" (true) rather than
   "no one does X" (unknowable). Numbers carry their source and year.

## What counts as unique enough

An idea is worth building when at least one of these holds and is written down with evidence:
- The specific buyer and transaction do not exist yet, even if the parts do.
- An existing model works in one market and has not been applied to this one (e.g. farm water credits to cities).
- The founder has access others lack (contacts, data, a channel) that makes the hard part tractable.

If direct competitors exist, say so plainly to the user and name the angle that differs, or recommend dropping it.
